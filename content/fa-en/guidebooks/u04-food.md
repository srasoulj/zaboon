# Food & tea

You'll order food and tea with Leila and join Maman Bozorg at the table. Practice saying what you'd like, praising a meal, and responding when your host offers another helping.

## Say what you'd like

Use <fa audio="">می‌خوام</fa> (mikhām) for “I want” or “I'd like.” Its negative is <fa audio="">نمی‌خوام</fa> (nemikhām), “I don't want.” The polite question form <fa audio="">می‌خواین</fa> (mikhāyn) asks “Would you like?”

Notice the spelling: the vāv in these forms is silent. Follow the recording and transliteration rather than trying to pronounce every letter. Adding <fa audio="">لطفا</fa> (lotfan), “please,” makes your order courteous. You can also simply name your order, as in the kebab-and-rice phrase above.

## Hungry, full, and happy with the food

<fa audio="">گشنمه</fa> (goshname) is the everyday spoken way to say “I'm hungry”; the written form is <fa audio="">گرسنه هستم</fa> (gorosne hastam). <fa audio="">سیرم.</fa> (siram.) means “I'm full.” Learn both as handy chunks.

Tahdig is the prized crispy layer at the bottom of the rice pot. When something tastes good, say <fa audio="">چه خوشمزه!</fa> (che khoshmaze!). Afterward, <fa audio="">خیلی خوشمزه بود.</fa> (kheyli khoshmaze bud.) praises the meal you just ate: “It was really delicious.”

## Ta'ārof at the table

Here's one possible exchange, not a script you must follow. Your host offers more; you politely decline with <fa audio="">نه، مرسی، سیرم.</fa> (na, mersi, siram.). Grandma encourages you: <fa audio="">تعارف نکن! بخور!</fa> (ta'ārof nakon! bokhor!).

If you want more, accept and thank her: <fa audio="">باشه، دستتون درد نکنه!</fa> (bāshe, dastetun dard nakone!). The thanks literally wishes that her hands won't hurt, acknowledging her effort. Your host replies <fa audio="">نوش جان!</fa> (nush-e jān!), “Enjoy!” If you're genuinely full, you can keep declining politely.
