# Hello & ta'ārof

You'll greet people, say thank you, and take your first steps in ta'ārof, the Persian art of politeness. Maman Bozorg offers you tea, and Shirin helps you practice everyday greetings.

## Spoken first, written second

Zaboon teaches everyday Tehrani Persian first. When the written form differs, you'll see it in a small “written” chip; both forms are accepted.

| Spoken                                        | Written                                                   | Meaning            |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| <fa audio="">خوبی؟</fa> khubi?                | <fa audio="">خوب هستی؟</fa> khub hasti?                   | Are you well?      |
| <fa audio="">اسمم شیرینه.</fa> esmam shirine. | <fa audio="">اسم من شیرین است.</fa> esm-e man shirin ast. | My name is Shirin. |

Persian runs right to left. Most short vowels aren't written: <fa audio="">من</fa> (man) has the consonants m-n, and you supply the “a.” Transliteration helps while you learn the letters. Start the Letters tab with five letters that don't join the following letter: alef, dāl, re, ze, and vāv.

Use <fa audio="">شب بخیر!</fa> (shab bekheyr!) when parting at night or going to bed, not as an evening hello.

## Two ways to say “you”

Use <fa audio="">تو</fa> (to) with friends, family, and children; choose <fa audio="">شما</fa> (shomā) for elders, strangers, or extra respect. The verb changes too. Ask a friend <fa audio="">خوبی؟</fa> (khubi?), but greet Grandma with <fa audio="">سلام، شما خوبین؟</fa> (salām, shomā khubin?). When unsure, choose the respectful form.

## Ta'ārof

Ta'ārof can involve offering, refusing, and insisting. Maman Bozorg says <fa audio="">بفرمایید چایی!</fa> (befarmāyid chāyi!), “Have some tea!” You might politely answer <fa audio="">نه، مرسی.</fa> (na, mersi.), “No, thanks.” She encourages you: <fa audio="">تعارف نکن!</fa> (ta'ārof nakon!), “Don't be shy!” You accept with <fa audio="">باشه، مرسی!</fa> (bāshe, mersi!), “Okay, thanks!”

This isn't a rule that you must refuse: you can accept directly or genuinely decline. You'll also meet ta'ārof in shops and taxis. A seller or driver may call the payment “nothing”; thank them and pay anyway.
